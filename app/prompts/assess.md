## What to produce

**traffic_light** — assess against the success measure as written, not against
how much work has been done. Effort and attainment are different things, and it
is the success measure the institute steers on.

Each judgment is written twice, at two lengths. This is not repetition — they
are read by the same person at different moments, and the short one is the only
one most people will read.

**traffic_light_rationale** — **at most $rationale_limit characters.** The decisive reason,
naming the adjacent value you rejected. One or two sentences. This is what a
director reads while scanning before a deadline, so it must carry the argument
on its own. Do not summarise; lead with the thing that settles it.

**traffic_light_reasoning** — no length limit. The full argument: what would
have to be true for the adjacent value to be right, why each component of the
success measure does or does not hold, and anything the director should weigh.
Written for someone who has decided they want to disagree with you, so give
them everything. Do not pad, and do not cut something that matters to be
brief.

**progress_percent** — an integer, 0 to 100, measuring attainment of the
success measure. If work has advanced while the measure has receded, the figure
follows the measure.

**progress_rationale** — **at most $rationale_limit characters**, and it must **state the
figure you are submitting**. If you derive one number and submit another, say
why in the same breath; a rationale arguing for a figure you did not submit
leaves the director unable to tell which is the mistake.

**progress_reasoning** — no length limit. The full derivation, component by
component.

## Two rules about references

Cite only claim ids that appear above; do not invent one.

**Every square bracket must contain claim ids and nothing else.** A bracket
holding prose looks exactly like a citation and leads nowhere, which costs more
trust than the note inside it was worth.

This catches people out most often when referring to the previous quarter's
submitted figures, which are context rather than evidence and have no claim id.
Write that in words — "the 45% submitted for Q2" — and use no brackets at all.
The same goes for `[see above]`, `[the meeting]`, `[context, not evidence]`.
